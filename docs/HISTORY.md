# Starweave -- full project history

This is the complete, session-by-session changelog that used to live inline in
`CLAUDE.md`'s "Current state" section -- every realm pass, every bug found and
fixed, every citation-audit finding, with the exact numbers and reasoning
behind each one. Moved out once `CLAUDE.md` crossed ~160,000 characters and
started tripping Claude Code's size warning. `CLAUDE.md` itself now keeps only
a condensed summary of this and points here for the detail.

**Read this before re-researching a node, re-opening a "resolved" decision, or
re-proposing an edge that looks obvious.** A lot of what looks like an open
question was already settled here, sometimes at real search-budget cost --
see especially the Search method / traps entries still in `CLAUDE.md` itself
for the behavioral rules this history exists to justify.

## Artist page room (Tiny Desk), in full

- **The artist page sits in a room, in the spirit of a Tiny Desk session (Oct 2026; `components/artist/backline/`: `Room`, `Bass`, `Guitar`, `Decor`; data in `lib/backline.ts`, string physics in `lib/strings.ts`, notes in `lib/song-notes.ts`, tests for all three).** The room is crammed like a Tiny Desk office (`Wall.tsx`; owner: thin shelves of even covers on a flat wall looked like "those google meet library bg.. cheap"): a wooden bookcase floor to ceiling in each side column, every shelf filled differently -- dense runs of record spines (a few leaning), their own and their family's records face-out, singles, books laid flat with cassettes on top (the About page's `Cassette`, a family member's name on the label), framed photos -- and the wall behind the page (a deep shade of their colour, `roomWall`) plastered with posters that show through every gap between the sheets: band posters printed in one ink over black from their family's photos with the name in Anton, records pinned up as promo posters, their scene's flyer (name, city, years from the map; no invented gig), and type posters of the genres they're filed under, all taped up. Everything pinned to the screen; upright apart from tape, leaning records and photos without words; seeded per artist. **On the big low hits, a few things rattle and settle** (`data-rattle` with their own `--rx/--ry/--rr`, moved by `Room`: a quick 11Hz shiver damped over ~0.13s): the records turned face-out and the leaning ones, the cassettes, the framed photos -- never the dense runs of spines (jittering stripes are hard on the eyes) or the posters (they carry type). Only when the low end is genuinely loud (`levelOf(0, 3) > 0.74`), a strong low note or a beat with the swell over 0.65, at most every 0.4s: measured ~3-4 jolts in 6s on Fugazi, Cocteau Twins and Slowdive, about a fifth of the time; an earlier, looser trigger rattled 92% of the time. Subtle on purpose: the band moving a lot and the room a little is part of what puts the band in front. On a phone the bookcases go and the poster wall shows through the gaps. In front, by the artist's sound (`kitFor`): **band** (most) gets a bass in the left column (body in their colour, cream pickguard) and an offset guitar in the right (cream, tortoiseshell guard, a ribbon in their colour tied to its strap; held high to keep the floating pocket player's corner clear). **They're held, not displayed** (owner: upright on a stand and a hook they "look like props sitting idle"): leaning in towards the page, **in front of it but never over its words** (owner: "a bit over the page... and not hurt the readability"): the band is its own layer above the sheets (`.room__band`, z 4; the shelves stay behind), and `holdAt` places each, leaning 15-16°, just where its inner side (`BASS_REACH`/`GUITAR_REACH`) crosses the page's edge by `OVERLAP` (20px), even mid-sway -- they sway freely outward but only `reach` degrees further in (on narrow windows `fitTilt` leans them less instead). The page's text sits ~23-30px inside its edges. Kept out of the words, they first read as part of the wall: nothing crossed the page's edge, their shadows fell down the wall, and they were lit like the shelves. So the headstocks now cross the paper's edge, their shadows fall onto the page (`--cast`), the room is a touch darker (`filter` on `.room`), and the page is a little narrower to give them room (`.artist-family` max 1060px). They step back (fade and drop) while the full-width credits at the foot are on screen. Each on a woven camo strap like Karly's running up off the screen to an unseen shoulder, turning about the body's middle where a player holds it. Both are drawn to real proportions, frets by the twelfth root of two, no maker's name. **Decorated like Karly Hartzman's (Wednesday) guitar:** pin badges of who shaped them on the bass (plain sewing buttons fill empty spots), die-cut photo stickers of who they shaped on the guitar (none if they've shaped no one yet). **When a song plays they're played:** a pick (in the ribbon's colour, hidden at rest) strums each note the song strikes -- a sweep for a chord or strum, a flick at one string for a single note, each stroke the other way -- and the string it hits shows as a real vibrating string looks: a lens-shaped blur with its two edges drawn, widest mid-string, dying away (bass the low half of the bands, guitar the high; a slow sway read as "moving thread").

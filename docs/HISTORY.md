# Starweave -- full project history

This is the complete, session-by-session changelog that used to live inline in
`CLAUDE.md`'s "Current state" section -- every realm pass, every bug found and
fixed, every citation-audit finding, with the exact numbers and reasoning
behind each one. Moved out once `CLAUDE.md` crossed ~160,000 characters and
started tripping Claude Code's size warning. `CLAUDE.md` itself now keeps only
a condensed summary of this and points here for the detail.

**Read this before re-researching a node, re-opening a "resolved" decision, or
re-proposing an edge that looks obvious.** A lot of what looks like an open
question was already settled here, sometimes at real search-budget cost --
see especially the Search method / traps entries still in `CLAUDE.md` itself
for the behavioral rules this history exists to justify.

Only the 5 scoring at 0.75–0.85 were re-scored this pass; **~38 remain at 0.5–0.7 with the shape identified but not resolved.**
  - **A content-free citation is where an unchecked claim hides — this is the pass's main lesson.** Seven citations named a publication but not what was said (e.g. *"NME feature/interview on IDLES."*). - **Genre secondary-lineage audit + `/genres` discoverability pass (Aug 2026).** The primary `parent` hierarchy had been audited across three passes; the `alsoFrom` axis added afterwards never had been, and had drifted into the same **bucketing** the primary passes existed to remove — three hubs held 15 of 41 secondary lines, with `singer-songwriter` accumulating five children that amounted to "anything intimate" (art-pop, indie-pop among them). Assessed on musicological grounds, deliberately NOT on the project's own edge counts.
  - **Two reparentings, each demoting the old parent to `alsoFrom` so nothing is lost.** `ambient`: krautrock → **minimalism** (Eno's *Music for Airports* descends from Riley/Reich/Satie and process music, not from Can and Faust) — this also finally gives `minimalism` a primary child and ends its long-flagged stranded-root status. `britpop`: indie-rock → **jangle-pop** (the Smiths→Blur/Oasis line is the real spine; `indie-rock` is defined here as *"American indie consolidating after Sub Pop"*, which is the thing Britpop defined itself against).
  - **Eight removals across two rounds:** `art-pop`/`indie-pop ← singer-songwriter`, `jangle-pop`/`industrial ← proto-punk`, `synth-pop ← industrial` (bucketing); `jangle-pop ← indie-pop` (a genuine bug — a **mutual pair**, both 1983, each claiming the other); `emo ← punk` and `lo-fi ← alt-rock` (pure duplicates of a direct chain).
  - **Ten additions.** First round, evidence-led (`no-wave ← minimalism`/`industrial`, `folk-punk ← punk`, `darkwave ← industrial`); second round on musical grounds (`alt-rock ← jangle-pop`/`hardcore-punk`, `alt-country ← punk`, `noise-pop ← psychedelic-pop`, `freak-folk ← psychedelic-pop`, `riot-grrrl ← hardcore-punk`). **`no-wave ← minimalism` closes a case pass 3 explicitly held** — that pass proposed minimalism as no wave's *child* and parked it because minimalism is 14 years older; the answer was the same relationship pointing the other way, and Branca and Chatham are minimalist composers and no-wave figures both.
  - **RULING: redundancy in `alsoFrom` is ALLOWED and this is settled — documented at the field in `data/types.ts`, read it before "cleaning up".** A dashed line to a *grandparent* asserts a DIRECT debt rather than an inherited one, which the solid chain cannot express (hardcore-punk lists proto-punk because Black Flag drew on the Stooges themselves; post-punk lists it because PiL and Joy Division owed the Velvets more than the Ramones). 8 such links exist. An earlier round of this same pass wrongly rejected `alt-rock ← proto-punk` (23 edges) and `post-punk ← proto-punk` (22) — **the two highest-evidence candidates in the whole graph** — on redundancy grounds alone; both were subsequently added. Only three checks are hard, and all read zero: no secondary postdating its child, no cycles, no genre listing its own DIRECT parent.
  - **Method note: edge-flow density is a filter, not an oracle.** Ranking candidate links by artist-edges-per-genre-pair surfaced good leads, but the single highest-ranked candidate (`power-pop ← proto-punk`, 0.185) was rejected — power pop descends from the Beatles and the Who, already covered by `psychedelic-pop`, and the density was picking up Big Star-to-Velvets artist overlaps. Small genres inflate density badly (2 artists can score high off one edge).
  - **Known-unfixable weak primaries, limited by vocabulary rather than judgment:** `trip-hop ← ambient` (really hip-hop and dub), `idm ← synth-pop` (really techno/acid house), `hyperpop ← idm` (really bubblegum pop and trap). **The vocabulary has no ids for hip-hop, dub, techno/house, country, glam, prog or folk-rock**, and that absence is the root cause. Adding them is its own pass — it means re-tagging artists, and a tagging change is three coordinated edits. Also left alone deliberately: `grunge ← alt-rock` (confirmed correct by the project owner) and `slowcore ← indie-rock`.
  - **`/genres` rendering: every secondary line now carries a direction cue.** Lines are drawn **ancestor → descendant** with a tangent-oriented chevron at t=0.62. Without it a mid-chain genre read as "connected to two unrelated things" — `art-pop` takes a line IN from post-punk and gives one OUT to dream-pop, and both arcs were drawn identically, which is exactly how the diagram was misread. A chevron rather than an SVG `<marker>`: `markerUnits="strokeWidth"` scales an arrowhead off a 1.1px dashed stroke to nothing, and per-line colour would need one marker def per root colour.
  - **A–Z discoverability.** The A–Z list starts below the fold, so a jump link (`.genres-page__jump`) sits in the header under the deck — in reading order, **not** in a corner of the hint row, where a first attempt at an outlined pill read as chrome bolted onto a poster and went unnoticed anyway. **It is teal (`--color-indie-alt`) for a structural reason, not taste: the timeline spends every other design-system colour on this page** — gold is the garage-rock branch, magenta art-rock, rose psychedelic-pop, olive singer-songwriter, grey minimalism, and indigo is both `DEFAULT_ROOT_COLOR` and the page's ambient background. Teal is the only one left, so it can only read as "interactive." Gold was actually *brighter* (12.0:1 vs 10.4:1), so the problem was collision, never contrast. The section border also went 0.08 → 0.16 alpha, because at 0.08 the sliver visible at the fold read as the page simply ending.

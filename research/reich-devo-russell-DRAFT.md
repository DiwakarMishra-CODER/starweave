### Placement proposal: Reich

- **Realm:** `post-rock-drone-noise`. That keeps the `minimalism` genre's members together, since Glenn Branca and Rhys Chatham are both there.
- **Lineage:** `drone`, the only lineage that isn't tied to a rock scene (Boris sits there too). Alternative: `electronic` / `ambient-drone`, since ambient's genre parent is minimalism and Eno's link runs straight into it. The owner should choose. I lean PRDN, because most of his descendants are rock bands (Wire, Squid, American Football, Deerhunter, Chatham).
- **Layer:** `root` (King Crimson is set up as `layer: 'root'`). Outside region-one the layer is only a placeholder and doesn't change the colour.
- **Fields:** `country: 'US'`, `activeFrom: 1965` (*It's Gonna Rain*), `scope: ['underground']`.
- **Genres:** `['minimalism']`. The existing root genre (1964, "Terry Riley, In C") fits exactly. **The genre page needs a rewrite, not just a new member.** `data/genre-pages.ts` `minimalism.deck` currently says "no Terry Riley, Steve Reich, or Philip Glass here", and `originParagraphs[0]` says "none of them are on this map". Both become false. It also needs a new section (for example "The composers") holding `steve-reich`, ahead of "Massed-guitar minimalism", and `steve-reich` first in `definingAlbums`.
- **Signature song:** the opening "Pulses" of *Music for 18 Musicians*. Wire's Newman, Caribou and Santiago all picked it. On Apple: the 1978 ECM recording (collection 1452312720) is a single 57-minute track, so the preview is its opening, which is Pulses. A separately tracked "Music for 18 Musicians: Pulses" exists on the Nonesuch *Phases* set.
- **Major album:** *Music for 18 Musicians* (ECM, 1978). It is his best-known record, and three of the speakers above (Newman, Snaith, Santiago) picked it. **On Apple Music:** yes, ECM 1978 (id 1452312720). Alternative: *Early Works* (Nonesuch, 1987; id 79577208; tracks *It's Gonna Rain*, *Come Out*, *Piano Phase*, *Clapping Music*, all with previews). That is the record behind the two strongest links (Eno, Chatham). It's a compilation, though, so I'd keep it as the second choice.

### Placement proposal: Devo

- **Realm:** `region-one`, beside Wire, Gang of Four and Talking Heads, the post-punk spine it belongs with. `american-underground`'s lineages (noise-alt, college-rock, indie-rock, psych, neo-psych) don't fit a 1973 Akron art-punk band, even though five of its six descendants live there. Cross-realm links are normal.
- **Layer:** `post-punk`. That gives it real indigo instead of adding another `outside` placeholder, and fits Wire and Gang of Four. `root` is the alternative if it's treated as an outside root like King Crimson.
- **Fields:** `country: 'US'`, `activeFrom: 1973`, `scope: ['underground']`.
- **Genres:** `['post-punk', 'art-rock']`, and possibly `'synth-pop'` (*Freedom of Choice* onward). Each tag means adding `devo` to that genre page's groups in `data/genre-pages.ts`.
- **Signature song:** "Gut Feeling / (Slap Your Mammy)". It's the song Pulp learned first, it's on the major album, and Apple has a preview. Alternative: "Jocko Homo" (same album, preview on Apple). "Whip It" is the hit, but it isn't on the debut and it's the mainstream face the outside-roots rule keeps away from.
- **Major album:** *Q: Are We Not Men? A: We Are Devo!* (1978). It's the album Pulp, Pollard and McCluskey name, and Mothersbaugh's Eno link runs through it. **On Apple Music:** yes (id 335252211; deluxe 337005954).
